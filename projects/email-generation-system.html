Automated Email Generation System

A Python system that populates PDF templates from Excel data and distributes them by email on schedule, replacing a fully manual process across four separate mailings and saving an estimated 15–20 hours of combined scheduler time per day, equivalent to approximately $40,000 annually.

Python PDF Automation Email Automation Excel Automation

The Problem

A manual process that had not previously been automated

I started that summer as a data-entry intern, manually filling in PDF forms from Excel data and passing them to schedulers, who reviewed and emailed them out to clients by hand. Several other people across the team performed the same work daily, across several different scheduled mailings, and the process had remained entirely manual with no prior evaluation of whether it could be automated.

The Approach

From spreadsheet to sent email, with no one in the loop

I identified the opportunity to automate the process and proposed it to my supervisor. The resulting system reads the same Excel data used by the manual workflow, populates PDF templates programmatically, constructs the corresponding emails, and sends them on schedule without manual file preparation. Once the first mailing worked end to end, I extended the same pattern to all four scheduled email types the team had been sending out manually.

Excel data → Populate PDF template → Build email from template → Attach & send

Results

Automating a recurring operational cost

Automating all four scheduled mailings eliminated a recurring manual workflow and saved an estimated 15–20 hours of combined scheduler time per day, equivalent to approximately $40,000 annually across five schedulers. The project also led to an extended role focused on developing larger automation and analytical systems.

Engineering Lessons

Reflections

  • Initiative matters as much as implementation. Nobody was going to hand a brand-new intern a project like this by default — identifying and proposing a viable automation opportunity, on top of the data-entry work I was already assigned, was as important as building it.
  • Defensive engineering matters in operational workflows. A mistake here wasn't a lower grade on one assignment — it was a real email going out to a real client, wrong or incomplete. The source data was often messy and missing values in inconsistent places, so most of the engineering was defensive: validating everything before it touched a template, and building in failsafes that would hold a mailing back rather than let anything imperfect go out.
  • Impact and technical complexity are independent axes. This was the simplest system I've built, and one of the highest-impact, because the value was in identifying an overlooked, repetitive process that nobody had prioritized fixing, not in the sophistication of the fix itself.
